# 1–2 · Decide the piece, then validate it

## Decide

**One idea per video.** A Short carries one; a longform carries one with several parts. A video
that carries two carries neither.

Write three things down before anything else:

- **Who is watching**, specifically. Not "beginners" — "a consultant between lists", "a junior
  dev who has used the tool twice".
- **The one thing they can do differently** afterwards.
- **What they already own** that the video can open on. This becomes the first line.

## Pick the shape from what performs, not from what you like

Before adapting somebody's video, check whether the video you are adapting actually worked. A
reference chosen because it looked good was **six hours old with 5.7k views** on a 129k-subscriber
channel — promising, but no evidence of anything. That channel's real breakouts were older and a
different shape: free-tool roundups, "better than 99% of people", "N hours in M minutes",
head-to-head comparisons.

Sort the source channel by popular, not by recent. Adapt the pattern that has run.

## Validate before scripting, not after

A claim that fails changes the script. Discovering it after the take is recorded costs the take.

**Primary sources only.** The vendor's own pricing page beats every article about it. The statute
beats every compliance blog. On one video, secondary sources were wrong about a product's price,
about a feature's tier, and about what a data-protection act contained — three for three.

Checks that have earned their place:

- **Vendor claims** → the vendor's own current page, loaded from the region your audience is in,
  so the currency and prices are the ones they see.
- **Legal or regulatory claims** → the instrument itself, or a law-firm country guide. Note the
  commencement date; a statute that is law is not necessarily a statute that is in force.
- **"Fastest/biggest/first" claims** → almost always stale. Check whether it was ever a first-party
  figure and whether it has since been beaten.
- **A claim you are adapting from another video** → check it independently. Two of seven segments
  in one reference rested on a tier claim the vendor's own page contradicted.

## Write the validation down

Keep a short file beside the script recording, for every checkable claim: the claim, the source,
the date checked, and the verdict. When a viewer challenges something a year later, that file is
the answer. It is also what stops the same claim being re-verified next time.

Mark anything perishable — prices, model names, tier boundaries — as needing a re-check before
the next video reuses it.

## Correcting other people, on camera

When validation turns up an error in a well-known source, that correction is worth more than the
fact. Say it plainly, show the receipt on screen, and date-stamp the capture. It is the strongest
move a small channel has, and it costs one shot.

Name the claim in a caption rather than pointing at it with an arrow — an arrow is only as
accurate as the coordinates guessed for it, and a mis-aimed one on a page offered as proof undoes
the shot.
